Chapter 14

Reviewing the Complete Manuscript

Chapter purpose. By the end of this chapter, you should be able to apply the central concepts to a real assignment, research paper, thesis, or article in your own discipline.

Learning objectives

  • Conduct a complete manuscript audit.
  • Check alignment, integrity, and consistency.
  • Separate substantive revision from proofreading.

Key terms

manuscript auditalignmentconsistencyreference checkquality assurance

Audit the argument

Confirm that the title, abstract, problem, questions, methodology, findings, discussion, and conclusion describe the same study. Track each research question through the document. Remove claims that lack evidence and evidence that does not serve the argument.

Audit sources and integrity

Check that every in-text citation has a corresponding reference and every reference is cited. Verify quotations against the source, preserve page numbers where required, and confirm that paraphrases do not distort meaning. Review permissions, anonymity, authorship, acknowledgments, and disclosures.

Audit language and terminology

Use one term for one concept unless a distinction is intentional. Define abbreviations once, maintain tense patterns appropriate to each section, and check tables and figures against the prose. For multilingual content, verify names, transliterations, translations, and Korean institutional terms.

Audit the document

Apply the required template and citation style. Inspect headings, numbering, captions, margins, page breaks, table continuation, reference formatting, and accessibility. Run automated checks, but read the rendered document because software may miss clipped text, broken fields, or misleading layout.

A complete-manuscript integrity audit

Conduct four separate reviews. The argument audit checks alignment among problem, questions, framework, methods, results, and conclusion. The evidence audit checks every factual claim, quotation, paraphrase, table, and reference. The language audit checks terminology, stance, cohesion, names, translations, and disciplinary usage. The production audit checks headings, numbering, captions, cross-references, margins, accessibility, and submission requirements. Build a citation ledger with one row per in-text citation and columns for source file, page or location, claim supported, quotation status, and reference-list match. Every referencelist item should be cited in the text; every in-text citation should have a matching reference. Move genuinely useful but uncited works to "Recommended Reading," and delete items that are neither used nor recommended. For thesis submission in Korea, compare the manuscript against the current graduate school template and departmental rules. Verify the official English title, student name, degree, department, university, committee names, approval date, abstract language, keywords, spine or cover requirements, file format, repository permissions, and correction procedure. Requirements vary; the authoritative source is the student's own institution.

Applications

  • Undergraduate application Use the checklist in this chapter to audit a final course paper and record five changes. Graduate application: Create a question-to-claim evidence matrix for the full thesis before final proofreading.
