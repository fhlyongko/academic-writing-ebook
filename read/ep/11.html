Writing Methodology as a Coherent Argument

Chapter purpose. By the end of this chapter, you should be able to apply the central concepts to a real assignment, research paper, thesis, or article in your own discipline.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

  • Distinguish methodology from methods.
  • Connect philosophical assumptions, design, and procedures.
  • Write a transparent methodology section.

KEY TERMS

ontologyepistemologyparadigmmethodologymethodpositionalityreflexivity

Methodology is more than tools

Methods are procedures for generating and analyzing evidence; methodology is the reasoned approach that connects assumptions, research aims, design, and methods. Selecting an interview, survey, or statistical test without explaining this connection reduces methodology to technique.

Philosophical assumptions

Ontology concerns assumptions about reality; epistemology concerns what can be known and how knowledge is produced. Positivist, post-positivist, interpretive, constructivist, critical, and pragmatic traditions make different assumptions and therefore support different forms of inquiry. Writers should explain assumptions only to the depth required by the discipline and design.

Alignment

A methodology section should show why the design can answer the question, how participants or materials were selected, how data were generated and analyzed, how quality was addressed, and what ethical safeguards were used. Qualitative studies may also require attention to researcher positionality and reflexivity; quantitative studies may require operational definitions, instrumentation, sampling logic, and analytical assumptions.

Cautious methodological claims

Use causal language only when warranted. Explain limitations without undermining the entire project. Methodological transparency allows readers to evaluate what the evidence supports. The objective is not to portray the design as perfect, but to demonstrate informed, coherent, and ethical decision-making.

Methodological transparency across designs

A methodology section is an argument that the selected design can answer the research questions ethically and credibly. It should identify the research setting, unit of analysis, participants or materials, selection logic, instruments or procedures, analytic process, quality safeguards, and limitations. Report decisions with enough precision for readers to understand what occurred; do not hide uncertainty behind technical vocabulary. Quantitative reports should define variables, measurement quality, sampling, missingdata treatment, assumption checks, model specification, effect estimates, uncertainty intervals, and any deviations from the plan. Qualitative reports should explain researcher positioning, access, sampling, data generation, analytic development, reflexivity, and the evidentiary path from data to themes or interpretations. Mixed-methods reports should specify timing, priority, points of integration, and the nature of the combined inference. Preregistration, registered reports, codebooks, analysis scripts, and data-management plans can strengthen transparency when appropriate. Open science does not mean releasing protected or proprietary data. The UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science explicitly combines openness with legal, ethical, cultural, security, and privacy constraints (UNESCO, 2021). State what can be shared, with whom, under what conditions, and why any restriction is necessary.
